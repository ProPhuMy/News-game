## Review focus

1. Seat recovery while the old socket remains connected: the replaced socket cannot issue new commands or receive new private snapshots; another seat cannot switch identity with a code, forged token or altered fields.
2. Same action ID across seats, retries and replacement connections: acknowledgments stay scoped to their authorized seat; retries do not repeat effects or expose another seat's credentials.
3. Hidden data at investigate, write, reveal, reconnect and final: every actual recipient gets only the allowed public data and its own private view; legal published evidence/final truth remain visible.
4. Resource abuse across connections and transports: existing room creation budgets, payload caps, receipt caps and expiry remain effective; identify limits not bounded by current code rather than claiming denial-of-service immunity.
5. Production startup and asset requests: private source/environment files never become HTTP assets; malformed paths and failed provider/configuration paths do not disclose secrets or prevent healthy rooms from progressing.
